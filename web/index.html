Where the data comes from

NASA satellites measure the heat of active fires from space: each detection is a heat spot (hotspot). There are two generations:

  • MODIS, since 2000, looks at the Earth in 1 km squares and misses small fires.
  • VIIRS, since 2012, looks in 375 m squares and detects 2 to 5 times more hotspots.

If they are simply put together, it looks like fire shot up across Europe in 2012, but only the satellite changed. That is why we correct the older years: during the period when both work, we measure, country by country, how many hotspots VIIRS sees for each one from MODIS, and apply that ratio. Each country has its own because its fires are different: large forest fires in the south, many small agricultural fires in the east.

Technical detail: one MODIS→VIIRS factor per country calibrated in (90% intervals by bootstrap over years; countries with little data use the European factor). NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 are calibrated against S-NPP. Each month is counted with a single satellite, never summed. Low-confidence detections and fixed heat sources (industry, flares, solar plants) are discarded.

How to use

  1. Choose an area at the top: all of Europe, one country, or draw your own. You can also click a country on the map.
  2. Read the map sentence: it says whether this year is burning more or less than usual.
  3. Explore: the Calendar shows when it burns, Fires compares a fire with earlier ones and the map shows where.
  4. Or ask with the button at the bottom right.

Words you will see

Heat spot (hotspot)
A point where a satellite saw the heat of an active fire. A large fire produces many.
Usual
What happens in a normal year (the median of previous years) over those same dates.
Corrected
Older satellites see fewer fires than newer ones; we correct the older ones so all years can be compared.
